01 — Context

A PDF can't show a product in use

Kibit needed to explain itself to B2B clients without asking them to install the app or sit through a live demo. The classic media kit fell short:

  • No flow — loose screenshots can't show plan → listen → read.
  • Ages fast — every UI change means new screenshots.
  • Wrong focus — the brief was the product, iOS first, not sales metrics.

The design question

How does someone understand Kibit in 2 minutes, without installing anything?

Answer: let them use it. A page that reads in seconds, with the real app flow one tap away.

02 — The AI-first approach

I decide what and why. AI speeds up the how.

AI as design material for the final artifact — not for decorative images. Direction, hierarchy, copy and quality bar stayed mine.

03 — Process

Brief → artifact → critique → adjust, until it stood on its own

  1. Brief as prompt audience, tone, what's in and out
  2. Content first one reader question per section
  3. Functional draft a hi-fi wireframe to find problems
  4. Real logic 7 screens with state, not screenshots
  5. Critique & edit my fonts, voice and icons over AI defaults
  6. Quality & publish contrast, touch targets, mobile

3 feedback rounds with the sales team · 2 days from brief to live page.

04 — The prototype

Not a slideshow: a simulated app with state

Tabs reset the journey; stacked screens keep history, so Back works like iOS. Side shortcuts jump to any section and show which one is active.

  • Two states for “Your plan” — shows progress without real data.
  • “Show tap areas” — fixes the classic prototype problem: nobody knows where to tap.
  • Smart download — reads the OS and opens the right store.
  • Real HTML — buttons and links, not images with hotspots.

Home, Your plan, Listen and Read — the core flow, recreated screen by screen.

05 — Key decisions

Understanding by doing, not by reading

Two ways in

Explorers tap the phone; people in a hurry jump to a section.

Value in 5 seconds

A concrete promise and three numbers in the hero.

Life moments, not demographics

Breakups, relationships, old wounds, no time to read.

Phone, wrist, car

Platforms shown in real contexts, tying in watchOS and CarPlay.

A fluid page

Most shared links open on mobile, so it reads there first.

Three languages

Spanish, English and French from the same page.

The team lead called it “spectacular”, and the sales team in Spain loved it. The page had no analytics, so impact shows in adoption and qualitative feedback — which is exactly what I'd change next time.

07 — What I learned

With AI, the bottleneck moves from making to deciding

The brief is the design

The first draft was only as good as the audience, limits and tone I wrote.

AI drifts to generic

The first copy sounded like any wellness app. Editorial judgment is the job.

Measure from day one

Next time: visits, most-opened screens and download clicks — plus a test with 3–5 outsiders.
