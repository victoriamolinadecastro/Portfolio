Kibit — Big ideas, in minutes

From research to a shipped subscription app, designed from a blank page.

Lead UX/UI Kibit Figma Research → Launch

Context & challenge

The challenge

Kibit is a reading and personal-growth app — book summaries you can finish in about ten minutes, built from short modules, key facts, audiobooks, and expert-curated plans. (The name comes from “key bit”: the essential piece of information.)

The company behind it came from the VAS world — value-added services billed through carrier portals. Kibit was its move into a completely different model: a real app, in the stores, with subscriptions, acquisition campaigns, and ASO. The challenge wasn’t just designing screens; it was designing a product for a business model the company had never operated in before.

Objective

The objective

Define and design Kibit from a blank page: validate the niche, shape an MVP, and deliver a launch-ready product that could compete in the app stores and convert through a subscription funnel.

  • Validate the niche — confirm the opportunity and positioning.
  • Shape an MVP — define scope from the ground up.
  • Launch-ready product — built to convert through a subscription funnel.

The solution

A focused reading experience, built to convert

A focused reading experience built around short, memorable “kibits,” with personalized learning plans and a clear path from first session to subscription. The product launched on both the App Store and Google Play.

Visual identity & assets

A cohesive visual system

Beyond the screens, I shaped Kibit’s complete visual identity: a scalable design system with reusable components and modals, plus a custom set of assets — avatars, illustrations, and store visuals. This kept the product consistent as it grew and gave the brand a distinctive, cohesive look.

Paywall system

Designing the paywall system

I designed Kibit’s paywall system — a single visual family adapted to different moments in the funnel, each with its own pricing logic and messaging. The onboarding paywall introduces the free-week timeline after profiling; the content paywalls use emotional, benefit-driven copy to convert during browsing; and the gift offer wall presents a special discounted annual plan. Same look, different conversion strategy at each touchpoint.
